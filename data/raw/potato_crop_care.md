## Section 1: Seed Tuber Selection and Sprout Preparation
Using certified disease-free seed tubers is a foundational preventive practice in potato cultivation because viruses and bacterial wilt can carry over across generations. Pre-sprouting (chitting) seed tubers in cool, diffused indirect light encourages short, sturdy green sprouts rather than long fragile white sprouts, supporting uniform emergence after planting.

## Section 2: Earthing Up (Hilling) and Tuber Protection
As potato stems elongate, soil is mounded around the base of plants—a practice called earthing up or hilling. Adequate soil cover protects developing tubers from sunlight exposure (which turns tubers green and produces bitter glycoalkaloids), improves drainage around the stolon zone, and creates a physical barrier against potato tuber moth egg-laying.

## Section 3: Late Blight Vigilance and Foliage Monitoring
Potato late blight thrives during cool, misty, or prolonged rainy periods and can defoliate susceptible stands rapidly if unmonitored. Scouting focuses on irregular dark water-soaked spots along leaf margins and undersides, as well as dark stem lesions. Preventive sanitation includes destroying cull piles of discarded potatoes, ensuring good ridge drainage, and consulting regional blight-forecasting bulletins.

## Section 4: Irrigation Consistency During Tuber Bulking
Irregular swings between drought stress and heavy watering during tuber initiation and bulking can cause physiological defects such as hollow heart, growth cracks, or knobby secondary tubers. Educational irrigation notes emphasize steady moderate moisture and stopping irrigation ahead of harvest so tuber skins mature ("skin set") and resist bruising.
