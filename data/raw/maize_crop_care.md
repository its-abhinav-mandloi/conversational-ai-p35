## Section 1: Vegetative Growth and Nutrient Deficiency Clues
Maize is a high-biomass cereal whose early canopy health strongly affects cob potential. In educational crop-scouting guides, nitrogen limitation typically shows as a V-shaped yellowing progressing from the tip down the midrib of older lower leaves. Phosphorus limitation often appears as purplish-red leaf margins in cool early-season soils, while potassium limitation causes yellowing or scorching along outer leaf edges. Soil testing remains the reliable way to confirm root-zone nutrient status.

## Section 2: Moisture Sensitivity During Tasseling and Silking
The reproductive window spanning tasseling, silking, and early pollination is the single most drought-sensitive period in maize development. Moisture deficits during silking can delay silk emergence relative to pollen shed, leading to incomplete kernel set on the cob. Maintaining uniform moisture without prolonged waterlogging around flowering supports even ear fill.

## Section 3: Scouting for Fall Armyworm and Stalk Integrity
Fall armyworm feeding in maize whorls produces characteristic "window-pane" translucent patches on young leaves and ragged holes with sawdust-like frass in the funnel. Educational IPM scouting checks multiple plants across a field diagonal, encourages natural enemies such as parasitic wasps and earwigs, and avoids broad-spectrum calendar spraying that harms beneficial insects. Severe infestations should be evaluated with local agricultural advisors.

## Section 4: Crop Rotation and Residue Sanitation
Rotating maize with legumes or non-cereal crops breaks disease and pest life cycles that persist in crop stubble. Incorporating or managing infected stalks after harvest reduces overwintering inoculum for foliar blights and stalk rots in subsequent seasons.
